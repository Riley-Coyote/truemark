I wrote a rule that swaps to the phone photo whenever a window is less than 1.1 times as wide as it is tall. Your pane is 1196 × 1306, so it got the phone photo. Any desktop window close to square does the same, and so does an iPad held upright. That rule goes.

02 · the hero

Five rules for the pedestal scene

The published site already does the important part: the photo runs the full width and keeps its shape, so nothing falls off the sides. The plan keeps that, adds the mist and the full-screen feel on wide screens, and never swaps photos.

R1The room runs the full width. All five vials on any window 600 px or wider.

The five vials and their labels sit in one box, and that box always fits inside the window with a margin.

R2The room keeps its own shape.

On a laptop or a wide monitor it fills the first screen, trimming a little floor if it must, never a vial. On a tall window it keeps its natural height, and the cards come up beneath it, as on the published site.

R3The words sit on the room's wall.

When the wall in the photo is too short for them (an upright iPad), the same wall carries on above the photo, blended so there's no edge.

R4One photo from the first frame.

No swapping when the window changes. Reload or resize and it is the same room, reframed.

R5The floor runs into the cards.

The mist and the floor colour carry straight on under the class cards. There's no line where the hero ends.

R6Phones under 600 px get their own framing.

Five small vials or three big ones. That one's your call, below.

Phones:

Drawn with the build's own photo (the crisp, graded room) and the real vial positions from the code. The words and cards are sketched at true size; the real page will match these proportions.

If the light warmed toward evening

The same room photo, from daylight to early evening. On the page the light would change so slowly you'd never catch it happening: daylight at the top, late afternoon by the lot number, golden by the questions, early evening at the footer. No dark section, no spotlight.

07 · your calls

Three things I need from you

C1

Phones: five small vials, or three big ones?

Both use the same room. Five keeps the whole collection on every screen. Three shows each vial almost twice as large, but the outer two only appear further down.

My pick: five. It's the same room and the same five everywhere, which is the whole idea. Each vial still opens its product page, with a tap area sized for a thumb.

C2

Lot number room: a photo we have, or a new one?

The single-pedestal photo above is already this room. The other route is to make a new picture from the hero photo itself, the camera moved in on the middle pedestal.

My pick: the photo we have. Its wall, floor, pedestal and window light match, and its open wall on the left is exactly where the certificate goes. If it doesn't hold up at full size, I'll make the new one.

C3

The light: daylight all the way down?

Daylight everywhere is what you asked for. The only other version I'd consider: the same room's light warms a little toward early evening by the footer. No dark section, no spotlight, just the afternoon passing.

My pick: daylight all the way down. The warmer ending is in section 06, if you miss the sunset.

08 · then

How I'd build it, once you agree

  1. Bring today's work together: the restored cards, the crisper room and the speed work, in one copy.
  2. Rebuild the hero framing to the rules above, with the phone choice you make. Delete the photo swap.
  3. Put the whole page in the room: remove the dark section and the sky, set every section on the room's colours, and move the lot number onto the single-pedestal photo with the mist.
  4. Check every window from 360 to 2560 wide, your preview pane included, in Chrome, Safari and Firefox. Count the vials at each size and film the scroll next to the published site.
  5. Show you the result side by side with the published site, before anything goes live.
